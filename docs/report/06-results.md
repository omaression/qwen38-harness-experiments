**H3 is refuted.** The identity adapter, which cannot change an output in exact arithmetic, produced one losing reply on each bug-fix split: resolved rate 0.952 against 1.000 on the held-out split and 0.846 against 0.923 on the dev split, with 0 gains and 1 loss each (McNemar p = 1.000). With margin 0 the `primary` gate fails and the adapter is rejected on both splits; on the dev split the single changed reply also lowers schema and thinking validity from the base model's 0.923 to 0.846. Both values are below the bounds of 0.95 and 0.90, so on this split the validity gates would reject the base model itself. The comparison itself marks the loss as noise: p = 1.000, and the report's warning states that at least six losses with no gains would be needed for significance.

**H4 is supported on the dev split only.** On the dev split the trained judge adapter raised balanced accuracy from 0.808 to 0.962 ($\Delta$ = +0.154, interval [+0.038, +0.269]) with 4 gains and 0 losses, and passed every gate. The gain is not significant by McNemar's test (p = 0.125): four discordant items in one direction cannot reach p < 0.05. On the held-out split the base model already scored 1.000, so the adapter could only tie or lose; one changed reply made it lose ($\Delta$ = -0.024, 0 gains, 1 loss, p = 1.000), and it was rejected on `primary`.

**Sensitivity.** The reports' minimum detectable effects at 80% power are 0.131 (bug fix, held-out), 0.210 (bug fix, dev), 0.064 (judge, held-out) and 0.177 (judge, dev). On the held-out bug-fix split, a true change smaller than about three of 21 items is therefore unlikely to be detected.

## D. Item-level analysis

The per-item rows (`--items-out`) of the four reports show seven discordant items in total and classify them as follows:

- **Bug fix, held-out** (1 loss): the candidate's reply to `longest-run` placed thinking both in the reasoning field and in the content, and its answer was not a strictly valid JSON object, so the patch was never executed.
- **Bug fix, dev** (1 loss): the candidate's reply to `parse-ratio` thought for 3,015 words, outside the 1 to 1,200 word window, and its answer was valid only under the lenient parse. A second dev item, `parse-int-list`, failed in both arms, each truncated at 16,384 completion tokens without an answer; it is concordant and does not enter the comparison.
- **Judge, held-out** (1 loss): the candidate's reply to `average-rating:wrong` was truncated at 16,384 completion tokens without a verdict.
- **Judge, dev** (4 gains): three of the base model's failures were format failures (on `first-repeat`, 1,225 words of thinking followed by no valid JSON object; on `parse-int-list` and `parse-ratio`, thinking that never closed), and one was a wrong verdict (`word-counts`, the reference patch judged incorrect after 7,227 words of thinking). All four involve reference items. `unique-in-order:reference` failed in both arms.

Six of the seven discordant items are therefore format or truncation failures, and one is a functional error. No bug-fix item in either arm on either split failed a test after a valid reply: the overfit rate is 0.000 throughout, and each arm's hidden-test pass rate equals its resolved rate.

## E. Cost

Mean completion tokens per item are reported and not gated. The judge adapter used fewer tokens than the base model on the dev split (766 against 1,784), and more on the held-out split (899 against 395), where the report warns of a 2.3-fold increase; a single reply truncated at 16,384 tokens contributes 390 tokens to that held-out mean. The identity adapter's bug-fix replies averaged 477 against 412 tokens on the held-out split and 2,625 against 1,852 on the dev split, where each arm's mean includes one reply truncated at 16,384 tokens (1,260 tokens of a 13-item mean).
